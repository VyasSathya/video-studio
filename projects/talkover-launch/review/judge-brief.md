# Brief (what the video was supposed to be)

A 20 to 30 second product-launch film for Talkover, a free open-source tool that gives Claude Code a hands-free,
two-way voice on Windows. The benchmark is a launch film by Apple, Linear, Raycast or Arc: it should look made by a
design studio, not like a screen recording or a template.

- The only product UI shown is Talkover's real floating panel (an orb that shows the state, a row of round buttons,
  and one caption line under them). Nothing else may be presented as product UI.
- The panel sits on a designed background with depth. The camera pushes in on one feature at a time.
- Features, in order: always listening (you just talk); it talks back (replies read aloud); you cut in by saying
  "wait" and it stops, then it picks up where you cut in; fully local (speech engines on your own machine).
- A few words of kinetic type per feature. Eased motion, one motion at a time. Cuts on the beat, 1 to 2.5 s each.
- Strong end card: the name Talkover, one line, and github.com/VyasSathya/talkover.
- Music only, no voiceover. Faceless.
- Two formats: 16:9 (1920x1080) and a 9:16 recomposition (1080x1920) for phones.

The orb colours mean: blue = idle, teal = you are talking, violet = Claude is thinking, orange = Claude is talking.
