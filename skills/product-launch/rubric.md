# Product launch film: blind judge rubric

You see only: frame sheets of the film (frames at every cut, after each title lands, and the end card), the
brief, and this rubric. Answer every item PASS or FAIL with the frame number that decides it. Do not guess
intent; judge only what is in the frames. A film ships only if every item passes and the overall score is 8 or
more.

1. Real product only: every screen looks like the same real app (consistent UI, plausible data); nothing looks
   invented, mocked, lorem ipsum, or drawn on top of the UI pretending to be part of it.
2. No intro card: the first frame already shows the product, not a title on a background.
3. One focus per shot: in each feature shot one region of the screen is clearly the subject (framing, crop,
   spotlight), and it is the region the title names.
4. Readable titles: every title is fully on screen, not clipped, not covered, high contrast against what is
   behind it.
5. Short plain titles: each title is at most 5 words, plain words, no hype ("revolutionary", "seamless",
   "supercharge", "game-changer") and no exclamation marks.
6. Legible UI: where the camera has pushed in, the UI text it pushed into can be read at this frame size.
7. Never a raw screen recording: the app is always either framed in a window or deliberately cropped by the
   camera; no frame is just the app filling the frame flat, unframed and uncropped.
8. On-brand palette: background and accent colors match the tokens in the brief; no off-brand colors, glowing
   halos, particles, abstract 3D shapes, stock imagery or gradients that fight the UI.
9. No generated people: no faces, hands, bodies or avatars anywhere.
10. No collisions: no text overlaps other text, no element covers a title, no cursor covers the word it points to.
11. Strong end card: the last frames show the product name large and an install or visit line, both legible and
    centred in a calm frame.
12. Vertical safety (9:16 sheets only): titles and the focal UI sit inside the frame with margins; nothing that
    matters is cut by the edges.
13. Depth and polish: the establishing shot shows depth (tilt, shadow, layered background) and the window looks
    lit and separated from the background, not pasted on.
14. Looks official: overall, the frames look like a launch film a well-known software company would publish,
    not a template or a screen recording with captions.

Overall score: 1 to 10, where 8 means "I would believe a good design team made this".

The gate is not this rubric alone. There is an approved reference film (skills/product-launch/REFERENCE.md: the
Talkover launch film, approved by Vyas on 2026-10-17); a new film must also win or tie against it in a pairwise
comparison, run in both orders by fresh judges. This rubric is advisory until a judge is calibrated on Vyas's
own approve/reject labels.

Reply format, one line per item:
`<n>. PASS|FAIL - frame <k> - <reason in at most 15 words>` then `Overall: <score>` then at most three fixes,
most important first.
